# Introduction

!!! abstract "Wired Layer 1 for Ethernet"

In most fields you are required to demonstrate expertise before becoming a practitioner. Because everything in computing and data communications is so new, we are not yet at this level of maturity. For this reason, some of the work and practices in our field leave a lot to be desired. 

Electrical work in Ireland is well regulated and is generally done to the highest possible standards. However, data cabling has been added as a service by many electrical contractors without the expertise or background knowledge to ensure best installation quality. When we are designing, tendering for or supervising a layer 1 install, we need to make sure that we are working with the right contractors. 

Care needs to be taken through the entire process. Planning is done with architects and service engineers, everything is written down and formally agreed, nothing is left to chance. 

A layer 1 installation is not a random assembly of parts. Generally, we want our contractors to install a wiring system. This will be a selection of parts from a single vendor where the system of parts carries the vendor’s warranty after the installation has been certified. Typically, vendor warranties are from 15-25 years, this is important. 

When we do the initial work on a site the location might be a building site, exposed to external elements, with many trades coming and going. Once the electrical contractor has installed the containment in which our cables will go, the raw cables go in. We call this first fix. We have to make sure our cables are not damaged or interfered with in any way as other trades on the site may not know anything about our installation. We will normally inspect installations at this point. 

Once the building is near completion, our contractor will terminate, number and test all cables. This is called second fix. The output from this will be an electronic copy of the test results for all the points in the installation. Values should be shown for parameters such as length, delay, resistance, insertion loss, crosstalk, return loss and the wire map should be confirmed. Once this is received and validated, you should get a commissioning certification and manufacturer’s warranty document. We will normally inspect installations at this point and may be required to approve them. 

In most of the world, we try to adhere to the standard ISO 11801 and in Data Centres, EN50600. In the US, you will see reference to the EIA/TIA standards. These are generally compatible, but we will only use the ISO/EN standards in our references.

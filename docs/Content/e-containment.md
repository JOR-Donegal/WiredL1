# Containment

One of the first things we need to talk about on any site is the containment our cables will go into. Containment is the general term for the cable ways we will use for all data services. There are many types, but typically the installation will be looked after by the electrical contractor.

We should be asked about cable numbers and sizing. A typical CAT6 cable is 6 or 7mm in diameter. If we put these cables into a 50x50mm trunking, we could fit seven cables horizontally and have seven rows, one on top of the other. We could in theory fit c.50 cables into this trunking.

However, we never fill containment, it’s supposed to be good for adds/moves/changes for 15-25 years. One rule of thumb is to never fill more than 60%. So for 50 cables, we might specify a trunking of 100x50mm. 

Do a search on line for the following containment types and know what they look like. 

1. DADO rail trunking is used extensively in offices. 
2. 20 or 25mm conduit is used for small numbers of cables (4 or 5). 
3. Cable tray is placed above ceilings and is commonly used in the ceiling voids of corridors in buildings. We need to be careful that any bends or turns are large enough 
4. Basket is a cheaper option than cable trays and is easier to fit. Some cabling manufacturers will not want you to use basket. 
5. Lidded galvanized trunking is the gold standard from our perspective. The cables are completely protected from either physical injury or from electrical noise. We would insist on this in an industrial site.

Externally on a campus site, the ducting will be placed underground and looks very similar to that used for storm water and sewage. IS370 defines the color coding and was changed in 2007. Data ducts should be bright green or grey and are typically 100mm in diameter.
 
Where we are providing ducts to carriers there are serious legal implications. You may be asked to sign over rights to such a duct with a wayleave agreement. You need legal advice before doing so.
